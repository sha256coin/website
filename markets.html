Before you use an exchange

Basic safety checks. They do not replace your own research.

  1. Check the address

    Type the exchange address yourself or use the link on this page, then bookmark it. Fake exchange sites and “support” accounts in private messages are common. The S256 project will never message you first, ask for your keys or seed phrase, or ask you to send coins.

  2. Check that it is S256

    S256 is a native coin on its own blockchain. It has no token or contract on Ethereum, BNB Smart Chain or any other network. S256 addresses start with s21, S or 8. A token with a similar name or ticker is a different asset.

  3. Start small

    Test a deposit and a withdrawal with a small amount first. Check the exchange’s fees, minimum withdrawal and confirmation count. Low-volume markets can have wide spreads and thin order books, so the price you get can differ a lot from the last trade.

  4. Keep your coins in your own wallet

    Coins left on an exchange are held by the exchange, not by you. Exchanges can freeze withdrawals, delist assets, get hacked or fail. Withdraw to an S256 wallet whose keys you hold.
