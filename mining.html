Mine S256

S256 uses the same SHA-256 proof of work as Bitcoin, so any Bitcoin ASIC can mine it. Point your miner at a pool, use your S256 address as the username, and you are mining.

Setup Guide

From a new wallet to your first payout in four steps.

  1. Get an S256 address

    Install a wallet or open the web wallet and create a receiving address. S256 addresses start with s21 (bech32), S or 8. Pools pay to this address, so keep its keys or seed phrase backed up.

  2. Pick a pool and a port

    Choose a pool above. On Sha256-Mining, pick the port whose start difficulty is closest to what your miner handles; a small miner goes on 50272, a large farm or rental on 50276. VarDiff tunes it from there.

  3. Configure your miner

    In your ASIC’s pool settings, enter:

    URL
    stratum+tcp://sha256-mining.go.ro:50273
    Username
    your-s256-address.worker1
    Password
    x for pool mining, m=solo to mine solo

    The part after the dot is a worker name of your choice, so you can tell several miners apart.

  4. Watch shares and payouts

    Within a few minutes the pool shows your hashrate. Block rewards unlock after S256’s coinbase maturity (200 blocks; the pool waits for 201 confirmations) and are then paid out with the next payment run.

Hardware

Any SHA-256 ASIC that mines Bitcoin mines S256: the block header and hashing are the same. GPU and CPU mining is not competitive. Rented SHA-256 hashrate works too; point the rental at a pool’s stratum URL.

Solo with your own node

Run sha256coind with server=1 and RPC credentials, then use mining or pool software that speaks getblocktemplate against your node’s RPC port, 25332. The whole 100 S256 reward is yours when you find a block.

Merged mining from block 17,500

From block 17,500, pools on other SHA-256 chains can merge-mine S256 at no cost to their hashrate. S256’s merge-mining chain ID is 598. Pool operators: see the pool integration notes and run S256 Core v3.0.1 or later.
